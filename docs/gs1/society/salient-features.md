# Salient Features of Indian Society, Diversity of India

## 2025

**How do you account for the growing fast food industries given that there are increased health concerns in modern society? Illustrate your answer with the Indian experience.** *(15 Marks, 250 Words)*

??? note "View Solution"
    Introduction

    The growth of fast-food industries despite rising health consciousness reflects the paradox of modernity: health awareness is increasing, but convenience-driven consumption is expanding faster. Urbanisation, changing lifestyles, globalisation and aggressive marketing have transformed India's food habits.

    Reasons for the Growth of Fast-Food Industries

    **Mnemonic: FAST FOOD**

    | Factor | Explanation with Indian Examples |
    |---|---|
    | **F – Fast-paced lifestyles** | Dual-income households, longer working hours and time poverty increase demand for ready-to-eat meals. |
    | **A – Aggressive advertising** | Celebrity endorsements, influencer marketing and children's food advertising create aspirational consumption. |
    | **S – Socialisation** | Fast-food outlets have become youth hangouts and spaces for peer interaction and celebrations. |
    | **T – Technological change** | Swiggy, Zomato, UPI and cloud kitchens have made food ordering convenient. |
    | **F – Foreign cultural influence** | Globalisation and Westernisation have popularised burgers, pizzas and fried foods. |
    | **O – Organised retail expansion** | Malls, food courts, franchises and Tier-2/3 city expansion increase accessibility. |
    | **O – Offers and affordability** | Combos, discounts, value meals and low-cost street fast food attract price-sensitive consumers. |
    | **D – Diversification** | Indianised menus such as paneer burgers, dosa pizzas and McAloo Tikki adapt global products to local tastes. |

    ### 2. Why Do Health Concerns Not Reduce Demand?

    - **Knowledge–behaviour gap:** Awareness of obesity and diabetes does not necessarily translate into healthier choices.
    - **Instant gratification:** Taste, convenience and emotional eating often outweigh long-term health considerations.
    - **Health halo effect:** Consumers perceive salads, baked products and diet beverages as automatically healthy.
    - **Structural constraints:** Healthy food may be relatively expensive or less accessible, particularly for working-class urban populations.
    - **Normalisation of unhealthy diets:** Frequent consumption becomes embedded in family routines and youth culture.

    ### 3. Health Implications: The Indian Experience

    - **Obesity epidemic:** NFHS-5 (2019–21) reports 24% of women and 22.9% of men are overweight or obese.
    - **Dual burden of malnutrition:** Undernutrition and obesity coexist within households.
    - **NCD burden:** Increased risk of type-2 diabetes, hypertension, cardiovascular diseases and fatty liver.
    - **Cultural transition:** Traditional diets based on millets, pulses and seasonal foods increasingly compete with energy-dense, nutrient-poor food.
    - **Economic consequences:** Rising healthcare expenditure and productivity losses.

    ### 4. Way Forward: Balancing Industry Growth with Public Health

    1. **Regulatory intervention:** Strengthen FSSAI regulation, nutritional labelling and restrictions on misleading advertisements targeting children.
    2. **Behavioural change:** Promote dietary literacy through schools, workplaces and public campaigns.
    3. **Healthy alternatives:** Encourage restaurants to offer affordable, nutritious regional foods.
    4. **Industry accountability:** Incentivise reduced salt, sugar and saturated fats and healthier product reformulation.
    5. **Institutional convergence:** Integrate nutrition with preventive healthcare under POSHAN Abhiyaan and Eat Right India.

    #### Policy Value Addition

    - **Eat Right India Movement (2018):** FSSAI initiative based on the principles of Eat Safe, Eat Healthy and Eat Sustainable.
    - **National Multisectoral Action Plan for Prevention and Control of Common NCDs (2017):** Promotes intersectoral action against unhealthy diets.
    - **ICMR-NIN Dietary Guidelines (2024):** Emphasise dietary diversity and limiting highly processed foods.

    ### Conclusion

    The objective should not be to eliminate the fast-food industry but to promote **responsible food capitalism**, where economic growth, consumer choice and public health coexist. India's transition must be from *fast food to healthy convenience*, without sacrificing its diverse food heritage.

    ### High-Value Sociological Thinkers

    - **George Ritzer – *The McDonaldization of Society* (1993):** Efficiency, calculability, predictability and control increasingly shape consumption patterns.
    - **Glocalisation:** Global fast-food brands adapt menus to Indian tastes, e.g. McAloo Tikki.
    - **Food as an identity marker:** Eating out increasingly reflects status, lifestyle and urban aspirations.

    ### Answer-Writing Strategy

    This is a **sociological paradox** question. Avoid writing only about the health hazards of junk food.

    - **50%:** Explain the industry's growth.
    - **25%:** Discuss the health paradox and Indian experience.
    - **25%:** Suggest solutions.

    **Distinctive value addition:** Use Ritzer's concept of *McDonaldisation* to demonstrate sociological understanding.
